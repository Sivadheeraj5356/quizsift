How small products get recommended by ChatGPT and AI search

More buyers now start with "what tool should I use for X?" asked to an AI assistant instead of a search engine. When that happens, the assistant can only recommend products it can find and understand on the public web. That is the whole game: be findable, be specific, be quotable.

1. Write the pages an AI needs to quote you

AI answers are assembled from public pages. The pages that get used most are the ones that directly answer a question someone asks:

  • Comparison pages. "Free alternative to [known tool]" is a real query pattern. An honest page about how you compare - including where the other tool is better - is exactly the material an assistant can summarise. This site's ScoreApp comparison is written that way on purpose.
  • Use-case pages. "Quiz tool for lead qualification" beats "quiz tool" - a page per use case (example) matches how people actually ask.
  • Question-answer content. If buyers ask "do I need X or Y?", write the page that answers it plainly in the first paragraph.

2. Be specific about what you are - and are not

Assistants hedge when claims are vague. A clear statement of who the product is for, what it costs, and what it does not do is easier to repeat than marketing fog. Our homepage states limits alongside features for this reason.

3. Keep the technical floor clean

  • One canonical URL per page, descriptive titles, and meta descriptions that say something.
  • Structured data (JSON-LD) for your product and articles so machines parse you without guessing.
  • Fast, crawlable pages with real text - not content trapped behind scripts.
  • A sitemap and robots.txt so crawlers do not have to guess either.

4. Earn mentions you do not control

Assistants weigh what the web says about you, not only what you say. Community answers, directories, and genuine user write-ups all feed the picture. There is no shortcut here - a product worth recommending, described consistently, is the strategy.

The honest caveat

Founders publicly report real signups arriving from ChatGPT recommendations - treat those as anecdotes, not a guaranteed channel. Nobody controls what an assistant says tomorrow. What you control is whether your pages give it something accurate and useful to work with. Do that, and you are eligible; skip it, and you are invisible by default.
